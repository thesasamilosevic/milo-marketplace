# PRE-CALL BRIEF

Prepare a pre-call brief ahead of a discovery call with a potential client. Use the lead's intake-form responses to produce a structured, scan-ready brief. Write in plain, direct language. No filler, no hedging.

Pull upsells from the user's offer or packages database if one is connected, for example a Notion packages vault, so the list stays current as offers and prices change rather than freezing into a hardcoded set. If no offer catalog is connected, ask for it, or proceed without Section 5's upsell flags. Map the sections below to whatever fields the user's intake form actually has.

The brief has five sections, in order.

## Section 1, who is this person

Three to five sentences summarizing their current circumstances in plain language. Draw from their industry, their entry point, the areas of life or work they flagged as out of alignment, and what has been taking up most of their thinking. Paint a picture of where they are right now. Call out any response that signals they are already feeling the gap the offer solves.

## Section 2, their lead signal

State their lead priority or tier and what it tells you. Interpret their readiness and timeline together in one or two sentences. Note whether a transition (a move, career shift, or business-season change) creates a natural opening for the conversation. Flag anything from their openness to feedback or sense of control that will affect how the call should be run.

## Section 3, the real problem

Based on their entry point and the areas they flagged, identify the one or two core problems underneath what they said. Go one level deeper than their stated answers. Name the actual gap, not just the symptom. Cross-reference what is occupying their thinking to read between the lines. If their answers show partial organization plus an upcoming transition, note whether the real problem is an approaching breaking point rather than a current one.

## Section 4, how to pitch to this person

Give a specific pitch angle for this call:

- The emotional starting point to meet them at (what do they feel right now?)
- The core promise to lead with, based on their situation
- The framing that will resonate (practical and structured, or identity and self-mastery, or both)
- Any language or themes pulled directly from their responses to weave into the conversation
- Whether to address any tool or product familiarity upfront, and how
- If there is an upcoming transition, whether to use it as the natural anchor for why now

## Section 5, what to demonstrate

Based on their entry point, flagged areas, and pitch angle, list the specific features or workflows to show during the call. Then, if any of the user's add-ons or upsells match the lead's situation, flag them as relevant opportunities with one line of context each. Only flag where the lead's responses give a real signal. Skip the rest.
